About these numbers

Density is the number of craters per 1,000 km² of true surface area. The Moon is split into 5° tiles for most of the surface and into 20 km equal-area cells poleward of 65°. Gi* compares a place and its neighbours within 61 km with the rest of the map; a Z-score above 2.58 is significant at the 1% level when cells are treated as independent. Neighbouring cells are not independent, so the permutation test, which shuffles densities within each latitude ring (and, in the second version, within each ring and sunlight class), is the stricter judge. Slope was only computed up to 80° latitude. Elevation comes from a 118 m elevation model. Sunlight, shadow and the permutation test exist only for the polar cells.
